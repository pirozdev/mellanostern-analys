<p>The 60-day Islamabad MOU ceasefire expired today, August 17, but the two sides agreed an extension before the deadline — confirmed by Middle East Monitor (Aug 12), PBS, Anadolu Agency, and Yeni Safak. That agreement now frames the coming 31 days: the extension exists, but its durability is contested from within Iran before the ink is dry. Iran's Artesh spokesperson Brigadier General Mohammad Akraminia stated on August 2 — a quote amplified on state-affiliated platforms this week — that Tehran 'fully exploited' the MOU period to import new military equipment and repair damaged systems. No equivalent US or Iranian declaration of permanent diplomatic breakthrough accompanied the extension announcement. → supports N5 without move</p>

<p>Jared Kushner held a meeting with Hamas political leadership in Cairo on August 16, his first direct contact with the group, to advance Trump's 15-point Gaza road map. He is scheduled to meet Prime Minister Netanyahu in Tel Aviv today. Netanyahu's rejection of the road map last week — a rare public break with the Trump administration — drew condemnation from Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and other Gulf states, who said Israel 'now bears responsibility for obstructing peace.' Hamas has structural incentive to engage with the plan, at minimum conditionally, to deepen Netanyahu's isolation from Washington and Riyadh; a formal Hamas public response before EOD 2026-08-31 is the primary measurable on this track. → moved N3</p>

<p>The Strait of Hormuz remains near-closed. UKMTO put the week-of-August-17 transit rate at 17% of pre-conflict average. Bloomberg reported August 15 that Iran and Oman have 'homed in' on a formal transit framework, with Iranian co-management of inbound traffic and Omani oversight of outbound, but two blocking issues remain: Iran's demand for transit fees equivalent to approximately 5% of cargo value, which the US has formally rejected, and Iran's insistence that Washington end its naval blockade of Iranian ports as a precondition for any reopening. Trump complicated the diplomatic geometry on August 15 by stating he intends to declare Hormuz 'US territory' following Iran's defeat — a formulation that Iran's delegation in the Oman talks has characterized as a non-starter. → moved N4, supports N5</p>

<p>The IDF confirmed the killing of Hezbollah Radwan Force battalion commander Ali Samir Al-Haj Hassan in a targeted strike in southern Lebanon on August 15. Hezbollah confirmed the commander's death. Israeli military operations in Lebanon have continued throughout the MOU ceasefire period as a formally separate front; the US-Iran bilateral ceasefire framework has not been extended to cover Israeli-Lebanese operations, and neither side has cited the Lebanese strikes as a ceasefire violation in official communications. → context only</p>

<p>Iran FM Abbas Araghchi stated on August 16 that Tehran 'has not decided whether to resume negotiations with the US,' days after a senior Iranian official told US News on August 12 that extension talks were not even occurring — directly contradicting the mediator-confirmed extension. The dual-track Iranian public posture — formal ceasefire extension through mediators, public non-commitment on talks through the FM — is consistent with a regime managing domestic hardliner pressure while preserving the diplomatic option.
