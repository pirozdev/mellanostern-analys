[FINAL_INSIDE_IRAN]

Iranian state media (IRNA, Tasnim, Fars) covered the June 17 Versailles MOU with a framing centered on national sovereignty and conditional engagement — neither triumphalism nor capitulation. IRNA ran the Versailles ceremony as a "historic diplomatic achievement" led by Pezeshkian but consistently embedded the qualifier that the 60-day ceasefire was temporary and that Hormuz remained under Iranian jurisdiction pending "fulfillment of US obligations." Tasnim, aligned with the IRGC, ran parallel coverage emphasizing that the MOU "does not constitute a nuclear agreement" and that Iran's nuclear rights under the NPT remained "non-negotiable." → <span class="context-only">context only</span>

The IRGC re-declaration of Hormuz closure on June 20-21 was covered by Fars News and Tasnim as a lawful assertion of Iranian maritime rights pending demining compliance — not as a violation of the MOU. This framing is significant: it allows the hardliner establishment to maintain a veto posture without formally rejecting the MOU that Pezeshkian signed. Iranian state media did not report the Bloomberg coverage of the re-closure as a contradiction of the MOU, instead presenting it as a procedural clarification within the MOU framework. → supports <span class="cite">N2</span>, supports <span class="cite">N3</span>

Coverage of the June 13 strikes on Iranian military leadership and nuclear researchers was handled carefully. IRNA acknowledged the Israeli strikes but did not report the names of killed nuclear researchers by the Jun 21 reporting window — a pattern consistent with prior Iranian state-media practice of delaying confirmation of IRGC or nuclear-program casualties. The framing of US intervention (Jun 14 blocking of Iranian counter-strike) was absent from IRNA/Tasnim; the ceasefire was presented as Iran's decision, not a US-constrained outcome. → moved <span class="cite">F3</span>

Majlis coverage of the MOU as of June 21 was limited to committee briefings and hardliner criticism. No formal ratification debate had been scheduled. Majlis Speaker Ghalibaf (IRGC-aligned) stated publicly that the MOU "requires parliamentary review" but did not schedule a floor vote. This ambiguity — formal constitutional requirement vs. executive implementation — is the core risk tracked in N3. Iranian state media framing does not clarify whether Pezeshkian intends to submit the MOU to Majlis or implement it as an executive agreement. → supports <span class="cite">N3</span>

IRGC re-closure of Hormuz on Jun 20-21 is the first institutional pushback.
