## DA Opposing Narrative

## Judge Ruling

**REJECT (partial):** The DA's core contention — that Vance's positive framing is inconsistent with genuine suspension risk — is valid evidence but insufficient to move F8 below 0.30. The Analyst correctly noted that Araghchi's conditional language preserves diplomatic flexibility for Iran, but the conditional threshold itself is new and explicit in a way that prior Iranian statements on the negotiations have not been. The DA's point that the retaliation appeared calibrated (no US casualties) is noted but not sufficient: calibrated retaliation still occurred, and Article 5 of the MOU remains in dispute.

**REJECT:** DA's suggestion to reduce F9 to 0.06 is rejected. The difference between 0.06 and 0.08 is not material (below the resolution threshold of observation) and F9 is already set at the minimum credible non-trivial probability. 0.08 is defensible.

**ACCEPT:** The DA's falsifier criteria (no suspension statement by July 2 + confirmed next session = F8 resolves NO) are precise and correctly stated. These are the exact resolution criteria. The report should be read with the understanding that if Vance's positive framing is accurate and no new US strike occurs, F8 is more likely to resolve NO than YES.

**Weekly accept/reject of DA's strongest objection:** The strongest DA objection this week is that the escalation scenario weight (50%) overstates the risk given the calibrated nature of both sides' actions. REJECT: The prior for escalation under an active US-Iran military exchange with a formal 3-day suspension-risk forecast (F8) justifies 50%. If F8 resolves NO on July 2, the scenario weights should be recalibrated to approximately 30/50/20 (Esc/Prot/De-esc).
