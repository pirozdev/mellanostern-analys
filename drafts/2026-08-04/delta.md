## Inside Iran

Iran's information environment since March 8 — when Mojtaba Khamenei was installed as Supreme Leader following Ali Khamenei's death in the February 28 US-Israeli strikes on Tehran — operates under a framework of managed legitimacy for an unproven leadership figure absent from public view. State media (IRNA, PressTV, Tasnim) frames the conflict as a defensive contest for sovereign rights: the Islamabad MOU's partial sanctions relief was presented domestically as validation of Iran's resistance posture rather than a concession; the MOU's July 7 collapse is attributed entirely to US violations of freedom-of-navigation commitments and failure to disburse the $300 billion reconstruction commitment before Iran's own obligations were due.

The IRGC's Persian Gulf Strait Authority toll operation receives no domestic framing as a revenue mechanism. IRNA presents it as legitimate sovereign prerogative: maritime security management and environmental monitoring services for which compensation is owed. Araghchi's August 2 characterization of Oman negotiations as in "final stages" (IRNA) is framed as Iran negotiating from strength, not from economic desperation under the Hormuz closure.

Mojtaba Khamenei's absence from public events since March 8 has generated no documented dissent in TIER1 sources. Iran's president's July 21 attribution to him of "greater access" in state strategy (Tasnim/Bloomberg) functions as a legitimizing narrative: decisions are sanctioned at the Supreme Leader level without requiring his physical appearance. State media avoids language framing the war as existential to the regime; infrastructure damage from US-Israeli strikes is framed as documented American aggression that obligates reconstruction compensation, not evidence of Iranian strategic failure. → context only
