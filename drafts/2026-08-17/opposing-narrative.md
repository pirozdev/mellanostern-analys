"Iran-US diplomacy is on a slow but functional track — the June 22 Switzerland roadmap and ongoing Oman-mediated Hormuz talks show both sides want a deal."

## Judge Ruling: ACCEPT

The DA's opposing evidence is well-grounded on all three pillars:

1. **Switzerland roadmap collapse (July 7):** The June 22 "60-day roadmap" broke down within 15 days — Trump declared the truce over July 7 following Iranian drone attacks on Hormuz vessels. This is not a "faltering" deal but a documented breakdown of the primary diplomatic vehicle within its first operational cycle. The Day 171 ceasefire "extension" is an extension of what had already collapsed once, not a continuation of an uninterrupted track.

2. **Hormuz at near-zero (Day 171):** IMF PortWatch recorded 1 commercial transit on August 9 versus 73/day pre-conflict. UKMTO's week-of-August-17 reading is 17% of pre-conflict average. Even the Oman talks cited as evidence of a "functional track" are blocked by two unresolved preconditions (fee terms and blockade lift) that have no agreed resolution mechanism. N4 at p=0.15 correctly prices the "functional track" hypothesis.

3. **Mojtaba Khamenei structural ceiling:** The new Supreme Leader is assessed as more hardline than his father on nuclear concessions — a structural constraint, not a tactical posture. Any analysis projecting a near-term deal should account for this institutional ceiling. N2 at p=0.30 (working-group session, not a deal) and a hypothetical "nuclear deal by October" at ≤0.25 (DA's recommendation, consistent with N2) reflect this correctly.
