## DA Opposing Narrative: Why F4 at 0.24 Is Overweighted

**Core argument:** The Analyst's Oman corridor optimism rests on a single unnamed-source Bloomberg item and a "preliminary agreement" characterization from August 26. Against this, the observable AIS record as of September 6-8 is unambiguous: Kpler records 12 commercial transits on September 6 — **60% below the YES threshold of 30/day**. Getting from 12 to 30 in 25 days requires more than a corridor announcement; it requires sustained hull availability, P&I cover reinstatement, and insurer confidence that IRGC enforcement will respect the corridor boundaries. None of these secondary conditions are in place.

**IRGC attacks CONTRADICT the primary indicator.** The Sep 5-6 IRGC attacks on commercial vessels described as "on the unauthorized route" are precisely the behavior that the sustained-halt indicator (the primary +20pp indicator for F4) was designed to measure. Those attacks occurred *after* the "preliminary agreement" reported on August 26 and two days before Baghaei's "final stages" claim. The corridor, if real, has not yet constrained IRGC naval behavior.

**AIS data divergence is not symmetrically uncertain.** The divergence between TankerMap (~21/day) and Kpler (12/day) should not be treated as "average the two sources." Kpler is the source consistently cited by JMIC, which maintained "severe" risk as of September 7. TankerMap's 21/day figure includes vessels JMIC characterizes as operating under military escort — which the resolution rule excludes. The Analyst's corridor optimism partially rests on the more favorable of two diverging data sources.

**The 30/day threshold is binding.** Pre-war Hormuz traffic averaged ~40 vessels/day. Even with a corridor signed, a return to 30/day within 25 days requires: (a) full corridor operationalization, (b) insurer risk recalibration, (c) charterer willingness to route ships through a lane adjacent to active IRGC naval operations, (d) zero IRGC enforcement actions on corridor-lane vessels. Each step is a compounding precondition.

**Judge-accepted position:** p = 0.12. The Oman corridor is a real and positive development (+2pp above the Sep 3 baseline), but it is a necessary and insufficient condition — not a sufficient one — for F4 YES. The Sep 5-6 IRGC attacks dominate the signal direction. Source rule tightened per DA recommendation to exclude US government claims without independent AIS corroboration.
